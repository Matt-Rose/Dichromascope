## Evidence

**Cone pigments.** Neitz, Geist & Jacobs (1989) tested three dogs behaviourally. Increment-threshold spectral sensitivity and colour-matching experiments showed two cone classes, and the dogs had a spectral neutral point near 480 nm. Jacobs, Deegan, Crognale & Fenwick (1993) measured photopigments in dogs and foxes and found one pigment at about 555 nm and a second at 430–435 nm, common across the canid genera tested.

**Cone proportions.** Mowat et al. (2008) mapped cones in beagle retinas with opsin-specific antibodies. S-opsin cones were 9.9 ± 1.7% of cones in the area centralis (flatmounts; 11.9% in cryosections) and 18.3 ± 3.4% in the inferior periphery. The central value is used, rounded to 0.11, because the viewer simulates direct viewing. Dogs also have far fewer cones than humans in their central retina, about 23,000–26,000 per mm² at the area centralis.

**Brightness discrimination.** Griebel and colleagues (2004; *Journal of Vision* 4(3):10) trained two Belgian shepherds and a German shepherd to choose between grey squares. The Belgian shepherds' Weber fractions were 0.27 and the German shepherd's 0.22. The press release compared this with about 0.14 for humans in earlier studies, so dogs discriminate brightness about half as well as humans in this kind of task. The midpoint, 0.245, is used.

**Colour discrimination.** Dogs reliably use colour. Untrained dogs even found colour more informative than brightness when the two conflicted (Kasparson et al. 2013). Their colour discrimination is sensitive to luminance and display conditions, though: in one study dogs failed to detect blue on yellow at matched luminance on a screen (Byosiere et al. 2019). No receptor-noise fit of dog colour thresholds was found, so the chromatic Weber fraction is the default.

**Spatial vision.** Dogs resolve about 5.5–19.5 cycles/degree in bright light, against 32–44 for humans in the same apparatus, so humans can resolve the same detail from roughly three times as far away (Lind et al. 2017). The viewer does not simulate acuity.

## Derived

- Chromatic JNDs in bright light: about 22. Luminance JNDs: about 25.
- Photon-noise knee: Y₀ ≈ 9 cd/m².

## Open questions

- A colour-vision light limit for dogs, which would make the Light dial calibration dog-specific.
- Colour thresholds suitable for a receptor-noise fit. The Neitz, Geist & Jacobs apparatus measured wavelength discrimination and could in principle be reanalysed.
